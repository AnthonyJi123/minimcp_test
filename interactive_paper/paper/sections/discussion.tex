\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Design consequences: the two-stage gate}
\label{sec:twostage}

The evidence converges on a two-stage design:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Stage 1 (prefill, effectively free):} a mid-layer
  ($\sim$60\% depth) last-token probe --- 20/45\,ms, before the first
  token, and the only signal that survives duplex fine-tuning \emph{and}
  both input modalities (\S\ref{sec:quadrants}). Open engineering item:
  score-scale calibration for threshold (rate) transfer.
\item \textbf{Stage 2 (optional, post-draft):} $\ptrue$-post on the drafted
  answer --- the strongest single signal (0.899); the draft doubles as the
  distilled escalation query and as the fallback answer. On audio input,
  never ask over raw audio context: run $\ptrue$ on the model's own
  transcript (``repeat-then-judge,'' \S\ref{sec:audioptrue}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Positioning vs.\ query-side routing}

Query-side routers \citep{ong2024routellm,ding2024hybrid,chen2023frugalgpt}
decide before generation from query features, and the big model then
answers the user directly. Neither transfers to full-duplex voice: the user
talks to one voice whose session state lives in the small model, and
query-side features are precisely the ``type shortcut'' our audit
quantifies (pool oracle 0.715 vs.\ probe 0.822 in-distribution) and LOPO
breaks. The signal must come from the answering model itself --- consistent
with \citet{llmrouterbench2026}'s attribution of router failures to
model-recall gaps. A head-to-head comparison of query-feature routers vs.\
internal-signal gates under distribution shift, on their standardized
protocol, is natural future work.

\subsection{Result injection: resolved by the same signal}

Step 2 --- returning the thinker's result into the live duplex session ---
initially looked like a research problem of its own: the streaming smoke
test showed the talker pushing back on conflicting injected results. The
quantified answer (\S\ref{sec:inject}) is that for \emph{gated}
escalations the problem largely dissolves, and for a principled reason:
the same low-confidence state that fires the gate predicts relay
compliance (comply .42 $\to$ .75 across gate-score quartiles; 1.00 on the
deployed wrong-model $\times$ correct-expert workload). The gate and the
relay are two uses of one signal. Where the state is confident, framing
--- not ventriloquism --- is the mechanism (authority framing .53--.79
vs.\ seeding's backfire), and the residual ceiling is the swallow rate:
an unconfident talker relays a \emph{wrong} expert at .64 with zero
pushback, so cascade accuracy is bounded by expert quality.

\subsection{Three failure species, one perceivability axis}

The accumulated blind-spot results organize into a taxonomy of
\emph{why} a model fails, ordered by whether the failure is an observable
event in the model's own processing. \textbf{Retrieval-type} failures
(``I don't know this entity'') produce an observable empty lookup at
prefill --- pre-answer signals work (trap fire .90).
\textbf{Execution-type} failures (``stuck mid-solution'') only come into
existence during decoding --- decode-time signals see them (math
entropy@16 .860) and pre-answer signals cannot.
\textbf{Metacognitive-type} failures (``doesn't know the question itself
is broken'') never occur as an event anywhere in the model's experience
--- like a schoolkid confidently ``verifying'' Goldbach's conjecture, the
model answering a false-premise question executes smoothly along the
wrong frame, and every internal signal is blind (FalseQA fire .18, no
score separation; \S\ref{sec:falseqa}). This mirrors the human
feeling-of-knowing literature, which finds metacognition accurate for
memory retrieval and unreliable for insight problems. The taxonomy
explains the two-stage gate architecture (\S\ref{sec:twostage}
rationale: prefill probe for species 1, decode monitor for species 2),
scopes the claim --- the pre-answer signal is a retrieval-failure
detector, not a general oracle --- and notes that query-surface routers
miss species 3 as well.

\subsection{Demonstration system}
\label{sec:demo}

The measured pipeline runs as an interactive web demo: live microphone
audio streams to the duplex model on a single H100, the end-of-turn
probe read costs $\sim$54\,ms against the balanced threshold, and
escalated turns stall, consult the expert, and \emph{speak} the relayed
answer through the model's own TTS head. Two demo-only extensions go
beyond the measured arms: the expert gains a web-search tool ---
real-time queries such as stock prices, which the v4 FreshQA refit
(\S\ref{sec:falseqa}) fires on at .80--1.0 across tiers, then return
live values no cutoff-bound model can produce --- and the loop accepts
\emph{backchannel-aware barge-in}: sustained speech over the talker
ducks playback, a $\sim$1\,s ASR pass classifies the burst, and
backchannels (``ok'', ``mm-hm''; an English+Chinese lexicon) return
the floor with zero loss while anything else aborts generation and
seeds the next turn. All measured arms in this paper keep
the tool-free expert and the turn-based loop; the demo will be made
available upon publication.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

(i) $\ptrue$ prompt sensitivity: one phrasing each (pre/post).
(ii) The audio arm is validated on real dialectal speech (SD-QA), but
scripted read speech and one dialect split; spontaneous conversational
speech (disfluencies, self-repair) remains uncovered.
(iii) Input-side scope: formula-symbolic content (spoken LaTeX) is
destroyed by \emph{any} TTS render before the system hears it, so the
audio arm is scoped to speakable content via pre-registered input-side
filters (\S\ref{sec:livecurve}); this limit is distinct from the
transcription-uplink loss, of which a stronger cloud ASR diagnostically
recovers 38\%. (iv) The introspection-collapse finding is MiniCPM-scoped
(two generations of one family); the pre-registered second-family test
now passes on a hybrid-Mamba architecture for mid-band readability and
frozen-recipe transfer (\S\ref{sec:nvda}), but the full matched-pair
attribution against its raw backbone and the live-loop port were not
repeated there. (v) The frozen test split is reused across signal
comparisons (signals were pre-specified; a fresh test set would be
cleaner). (vi) Labels come from a single judge family, bounded by a
stronger re-judge (deltas $\le$0.010) but without human labels.
(vii) The live sweep (\S\ref{sec:live}) is one session per query per
tier, with a documented replication-noise floor of $\pm$.02--.03 per
arm; no claim rests on sub-3-point live deltas. The measured sweep ran
with text-mode talker output (\texttt{generate\_audio=False}): the
relay is validated at the utterance level and latencies are
text-complete --- a conservative accounting, since in the spoken system
the stall filler plays the moment the gate fires; the demo system
(\S\ref{sec:demo}) speaks through the native TTS head without touching
the probe context or the frozen thresholds. The native duplex
listen/speak head remains unused throughout: the probe is calibrated on
simplex streaming states, and its validity under the true duplex loop
is an open experiment.

\paragraph{Future work.} In rough order of leverage: (a) an
audio-direct-to-expert live arm --- the gold-inject view prices the
transcription channel at $-$.175 on the internal pool, an order above
the noise floor, and it is the one lever a single live sweep can
resolve; (b) true duplex integration --- driving the native
listen/speak head and re-validating the L22 read under the duplex
loop; (c) the Nemotron live port and its trained-checkpoint ablation,
completing the second-family replication; (d) a \emph{trained} gate
head as an upper-bound contrast for the zero-training claim; (e) the
remaining ten SD-QA dialect splits as a cross-dialect robustness
table; (f) protocol-level comparison against preference-trained
routers on LLMRouterBench \citep{llmrouterbench2026}; (g) a
$\ptrue$ prompt-sensitivity sweep; and (h) an injection-payload study
(does ``answer + one-sentence justification'' restore math relay
compliance where bare numbers fail).

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Zero-training self-knowledge in a small full-duplex model exists, is
strong, and is modality- and layer-fragile in systematic,
duplex-fine-tune-induced ways. The standard readouts --- final-layer probes
and verbalized self-evaluation --- each have one blind modality, and both
blind spots are created by the duplex fine-tune; the mid-layer
representation underneath survives everything we tested. Read there, the
resulting gate decides before the first output token, transfers from cheap
text calibration to speech on end-to-end models, and as a system dominates
the model's own thinking mode on both accuracy and latency. And when the
thinker's answer comes back, the same signal that fired the escalation
predicts that the talker will actually say it: run live end-to-end, the
gated duplex system climbs monotonically from its honest streaming floor
(.422 $\to$ .633 at 57\% escalation on the speakable subset), with the
remaining gap to the offline curve itemized into named, measured taxes
rather than left as mystery --- and the frozen gate transfers unchanged
to six public speech benchmarks, where at 50\% escalation it lifts the
9B system past models 3$\times$ its size. Gate and relay are one mechanism; what remains is engineering
the channel around it.
