\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Full-duplex real-time dialogue.}
End-to-end spoken dialogue models now span native duplex designs ---
dGSLM \citep{nguyen2023generative}, Moshi with its Inner Monologue
\citep{defossez2024moshi}, GLM-4-Voice \citep{zeng2024glm4voice},
and single-LLM native full-duplex \citep{bayling2026duplex} ---
streaming omni models such as Qwen2.5-Omni \citep{xu2025qwen25omni} and
our target MiniCPM-o~4.5 \citep{yao2024minicpmv,openbmb2026minicpmo}, a
9B omni/duplex fine-tune of Qwen3-8B \citep{yang2025qwen3}, alongside
frozen-backbone adapters (Freeze-Omni, \citealp{wang2024freezeomni})
and audio-understanding fine-tunes \citep{chu2024qwen2audio}. A
parallel line adds explicit \emph{turn-taking control}: FlexDuo
\citep{liao2025flexduo} drives a Speak/Listen/Idle FSM at 120\,ms
ticks, MinMo \citep{chen2025minmo} and Freeze-Omni attach lightweight
state heads over the LLM's own hidden states, and recent work analyzes
synchronization and turn-taking behavior in duplex models directly
\citep{sync2026turntaking}; \citet{chrono2026thinking} interleave
hidden reasoning with speech inside the duplex loop. All of these
decide \emph{when the local model should speak}; our gate decides
\emph{whether the local model should be the one answering at all}. The
two decisions are orthogonal and mechanically compatible --- the
head-based controllers read the same hidden states our gate does, and
their arbitration cadences (120--250\,ms) show a live duplex loop
already tolerates far more decision latency than our $\sim$30\,ms
end-of-turn read. Their shared choice of a \emph{last-layer} readout
is one point on our per-layer curve --- the point our results show
duplex fine-tuning specifically damages (\S\ref{sec:duplex}). We use
this model landscape as a controlled independent variable: matched
pairs across raw backbones, audio-only, streaming-omni, duplex, and
adapter designs attribute the readout damage to duplex-ness
specifically.

\paragraph{Does the model know what it knows?}
\citet{kadavath2022language} introduced $\ptrue$ self-evaluation;
\citet{lin2022teaching} trained verbalized uncertainty. A parallel line
reads correctness from internal states with linear probes
\citep{alain2017understanding,azaria2023internal,marks2024geometry,
burns2023discovering}, and intermediate layers carry more truthfulness
information than the output distribution reveals \citep{orgad2024llms}
--- consonant with our mid-layer findings. Semantic entropy
\citep{kuhn2023semantic,farquhar2024detecting} needs multiple sampled
generations, which a real-time loop cannot afford; Semantic Entropy
Probes \citep{kossen2024semantic} amortize it into a cheap hidden-state
probe --- the closest signal to ours, but read on text-only,
half-duplex models after the fact, not at end-of-turn inside a
streaming session. Multimodal uncertainty estimation
\citep{umui2026} asks whether confidence can be fused across
modalities; we ask the prior question of \emph{where} in a duplex
model a modality-robust confidence read survives at all. Our
contribution to this literature is not a new signal but a controlled
account of when the standard readouts break: probe and $\ptrue$
readouts fail in complementary, modality- and architecture-specific
ways under duplex fine-tuning, while the underlying information
survives mid-network.

\paragraph{Selective escalation and routing.}
Cost-quality routing across LLMs is well studied: FrugalGPT
\citep{chen2023frugalgpt} cascades with a learned scorer, HybridLLM
\citep{ding2024hybrid} and RouteLLM \citep{ong2024routellm} train query
routers, RouterBench \citep{hu2024routerbench} standardizes evaluation,
and \citet{llmrouterbench2026} report that many routers fail to beat
simple baselines under a unified protocol. AutoMix
\citep{aggarwal2024automix} is the closest system design --- a small
model decides its own answer's reliability and escalates --- but the
decision is \emph{post-answer} self-verification on text; our gate
fires \emph{pre-answer}, before the first output token, which is what
a voice deadline requires. Confidence-based cascades also appear at
the serving layer \citep{hybridserve2025}. These routers decide from
query-side features, and the large model then answers the user
directly; both assumptions fail in full-duplex voice, where the user
talks to one voice whose session state lives in the small model. Our
audit (\S\ref{sec:rq1}) quantifies the query-side ceiling: it is the
``type shortcut'' whose transfer collapses under distribution shift,
while the internal mid-layer signal transfers. Learning-to-defer
\citep{madras2018predict} and early exit \citep{schuster2022confident}
make the same decision inside one model; speculative decoding
\citep{leviathan2023fast} inverts our roles, and our prefetch analysis
(\S\ref{sec:liveloop}) is its query-level mirror. Escalating to the
model's own thinking mode connects to test-time compute
\citep{guo2025deepseekr1}; we find it dominated by external escalation
because the flagged failures are knowledge-bound, not compute-bound.

\paragraph{The gap.}
Duplex work decides \emph{when to speak}; routing work decides
\emph{which model} from the query surface or after a first answer;
uncertainty work asks \emph{whether the model is confident}, almost
always in text and offline. To our knowledge, no prior work asks
whether a full-duplex speech model exposes a \emph{pre-answer,
modality-robust, internal} competence signal early enough to drive a
live small-to-large handoff while preserving the ongoing conversational
session --- the regime this paper measures end-to-end, from signal
(\S\ref{sec:rq1}--\ref{sec:duplex}) to live system (\S\ref{sec:live})
to frozen transfer (\S\ref{sec:transfer}).

\paragraph{Evaluation practice.}
Query pools come from public benchmarks only: SimpleQA
\citep{wei2024simpleqa} for confidently-wrong long-tail traps (cf.\
PopQA, \citealp{mallen2023popqa}), MMLU-Pro \citep{wang2024mmlupro},
TriviaQA \citep{joshi2017triviaqa}, GSM8K \citep{cobbe2021gsm8k},
MATH-500 \citep{hendrycks2021math,lightman2024lets}, and Dolly/Alpaca
instruction data \citep{conover2023dolly,taori2023alpaca}; adequacy
labels come from an LLM judge \citep{zheng2023judging} validated by a
stronger re-judge (agreement 0.962 text / 0.945 audio). The audio arm
TTS-renders the frozen pool, following Spoken-SQuAD
\citep{li2018spokensquad} and VoiceBench \citep{chen2024voicebench},
and the audio-side findings are validated on \emph{real} human speech
via SD-QA \citep{faisal2021sdqa} (\S\ref{sec:speech}) and the live
external benchmarks of \S\ref{sec:transfer}.
