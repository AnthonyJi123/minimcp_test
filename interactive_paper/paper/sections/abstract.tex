\begin{abstract}
Full-duplex speech models answer in real time with a small on-device
backbone, yet many queries exceed its competence. Routing hard
queries to a large cloud model sounds solved --- until one asks
\emph{when} the decision must be made: before the first output token,
or the wrong answer is already on the air. We ask whether the model's
own hidden state carries that warning early enough, and find that the
obvious readout is exactly what duplex fine-tuning corrupts:
final-layer probes collapse under distribution shift on text input
(leave-one-pool-out math AUC 0.93$\to$0.37), consistent with late-layer
specialization for duplex turn control, while verbalized
self-assessment collapses on audio input. The signal is not gone ---
it sits one layer band earlier. Mid-network, the same linear probe
transfers at 0.93, reads speech input from text-only calibration at
0.86, and an end-of-turn read in the streaming loop reaches AUC 0.887
in $\sim$30\,ms --- half a second before the first audible word. Run
live end-to-end in a turn-based loop, the gated system climbs from
42.2\% to 63.3\% heard accuracy at 57\% escalation, the remaining gap
dominated by the transcription uplink rather than the gate; frozen,
the same gate improves five of six public speech benchmarks, lifting
Speech TriviaQA to 86.0\% live against the model's official offline
75.5\%. Duplex training breaks the obvious competence readout; reading
one band earlier, before speech commits, is enough to escalate live.
\end{abstract}
