\begin{abstract}
Real-time speech systems often rely on compact models, but some user
queries require a stronger expert. Selective escalation is well studied
for text, yet speech adds a deployment question: does a duplex-trained
model expose a reliable competence signal before it commits to a
response, and does that signal survive distribution and modality shift?
We compare decode-time uncertainty, verbalized self-assessment, and
linear probes across layers and matched model controls, using
leave-one-query-pool-out evaluation, matched text and speech inputs,
and end-to-end escalation loops. On MiniCPM-o~4.5, an in-distribution
final-layer probe scores AUC 0.822 but falls to 0.372 on held-out math
with text input; the same last-token read at layer 22 reaches 0.931,
while the cause of the late-layer failure remains unresolved. A
text-calibrated layer-22 probe reads speech-input states at AUC 0.855,
and a single-position end-of-turn study reaches 0.887 in
$\sim$30\,ms, before the first output token. The live system uses the
same layer with a separately calibrated three-position probe. In the
primary turn-based, audio-input/text-output evaluation, selective
escalation raises deployed-channel answer accuracy from 42.2\% to
63.3\% at 57\% escalation and beats matched-rate random escalation by
7.2--9.5 points; a separate TTS-on replay leaves gate decisions
unchanged when speech synthesis is enabled. With its weights, features,
and layer fixed, the gate also
improves all five external speech-QA benchmarks over always-local
operation, although it does not beat matched-rate random escalation on
every pool. These results identify a mid-network representation, rather
than the conventional final-layer read, as a practical signal for
handoff before response commitment.
\end{abstract}
