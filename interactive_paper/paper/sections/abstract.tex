\begin{abstract}
Full-duplex speech models answer in real time with a small on-device
backbone, yet many queries exceed its competence. We ask whether such a
model exposes --- with \emph{zero additional training} --- a pre-answer,
modality-robust internal signal reliable enough to hand a query off to a
large cloud model mid-conversation, without breaking the live session.
On MiniCPM-o~4.5 we find the standard readouts each fail in a systematic
way: duplex fine-tuning re-purposes the late-layer last-token position
for turn control, breaking final-layer probes on text input, while
verbalized self-evaluation collapses on audio input. The information
survives underneath: a mid-network linear probe transfers from cheap
text calibration to speech, decides at end-of-turn in $\sim$30\,ms ---
before the first output token (AUC .887) --- and the same
low-confidence state predicts that the talker will actually relay the
expert's answer, so escalation and injection are one signal. Run live
end-to-end, the gated duplex system climbs monotonically from its
honest streaming floor (.422 $\to$ .633 accuracy at 57\% escalation),
with the gap to offline operation itemized into measured audio,
streaming, and speech-channel taxes. The gate then transfers
\emph{frozen} to six public speech benchmarks using only label-free
threshold quantiles --- lifting Speech TriviaQA to .860 live against an
official offline anchor of .755 --- and its mid-network readability
replicates on an architecturally disjoint second duplex family. Duplex
models carry a free, readable competence signal that standard readouts
miss; read correctly, it turns a 9B talker into a system that knows
when to hand off.
\end{abstract}
