\section{The Gate Goes Live: Streaming Deployment and Result Injection}
\label{sec:live}

Everything so far measures signals in replay. This section closes the loop
--- the original third research question: once the gate has decided the
answer belongs to the big model, how does the big model's result get back
into the small model so the \emph{talker} speaks it? We run the full
duplex session live: audio streamed in 1\,s chunks, the mid-layer gate
read during streaming, the expert called mid-conversation, its result
injected into the same session. Three questions structure the section:
where the streaming gate should read (\S\ref{sec:liveloop}), whether the
talker will actually say what the expert answered (\S\ref{sec:inject}),
and what the tradeoff curve looks like when every oracle shortcut is
removed (\S\ref{sec:livecurve}).

\subsection{The streaming loop: the end-of-turn read decides, prefetch dies}
\label{sec:liveloop}

The offline gate reads $h_{\text{prompt}}$ --- the last prompt token's
mid-layer state. Streaming has no such token until the turn ends, and
per-chunk probe scores are strictly weaker than the offline reference
(best chunk statistic AUC .764 vs.\ offline .843), with a structural
failure on trap: short utterances back-load the critical entity into the
zero-padded tail chunk, and the mid-stream gate fired on only 1/5 of trap
(live) where the offline read caught nearly all. The fix is the
\textbf{end-of-turn read}: after the last audio chunk, prefill an
assistant turn containing a single space and read L22 at the
assistant-start position --- the streaming analogue of
$h_{\text{prompt}}$. This read reaches \textbf{AUC .887}, \emph{above}
the offline full-prefill reference (.843), restores trap
fire@balanced from .20 to \textbf{.90}, costs 22\,ms (still before the
first output token), and becomes the \emph{only} decision point ---
escalation-rate thresholds are quantiles of this single score, so the
chunk-vs-turn joint-calibration problem disappears.

The remaining role for mid-stream firing would be \emph{speculative}
expert prefetch --- start the cloud call early, veto at end of turn. We
kill this with a measurement before building it. Framing the design as
\emph{speculative escalation} --- speculative decoding's
draft--verify skeleton \citep{leviathan2023fast} applied to the
escalation decision itself, at sentence granularity --- its acceptance
rate is the probability that an expert answer
to the \emph{partial} transcript answers the full question --- a wait-$k$
style tradeoff \citep{ma2019stacl}. Judged on calibration texts truncated
at 25/50/75\% of the words, acceptance is \textbf{.07/.19/.51}; at the
chunk gate's typical fire point (27--40\% of the audio) it is
$\sim$.2--.4, i.e.\ 6--8 of every 10 speculative calls would be wasted.
Trap's acceptance floor (.00/.10/.27) is the same back-loaded-core
property that broke the mid-stream probe --- one underlying property,
measured independently in the hidden states and in the semantics.
Prefetch is dropped; the expert starts at end of turn.

Two more deployment-honesty decisions close the loop's design. First, the
expert query is the talker's \emph{own} transcription of the audio
(P50 2.2\,s, overlapped with the stall phrase), not the dataset's gold
text. Second, expert reasoning effort is \textbf{fixed at low}: on the
gate-escalated population, effort buys \emph{zero} accuracy (gold-text
queries: .85 medium vs.\ .82 low overall; math 1.00 at both) while
doubling latency (P50 5.8$\to$9.1\,s, P95 23.9$\to$78.8\,s) --- escalated
failures are knowledge-bound at both scales, mirroring the talker-side
thinking result (\S\ref{sec:thinking}). A planned deadline-aware effort
policy collapsed, by measurement, to the simplest possible one. The stall
phrase itself must be injected as \emph{assistant-role prefill}: the
official \texttt{teacher\_forcing\_text} hook continues generating freely
mid-stream, so canned filler semantics survive only if the phrase enters
as already-spoken assistant speech.

\subsection{Will the talker say it? The pushback problem, quantified}
\label{sec:inject}

The streaming smoke test surfaced the substantive step-2 obstacle: the
talker \emph{pushes back} on injected expert results that conflict with
its own reasoning. Before any live sweep, we quantified this channel on
172 live audio sessions: conflicting ``expert answers'' fabricated by
within-pool derangement of reference answers (deterministic, no LLM
generation), four framings from neutral (F0) through the deployed
authority template (F1), strong-override (F2), and assistant-seeding (F3,
teacher-forcing ``the answer is: X'' into the talker's own mouth), judged
into \{comply, pushback, lip-service, other\}. Five results:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The deployed channel is clean.} On the actual live workload
  --- model wrong, correct answer injected --- comply $=1.00$ ($n{=}24$).
  The live curve of \S\ref{sec:livecurve} is not contaminated by relay
  refusal.
\item \textbf{The same signal that triggers escalation predicts
  compliance.} Across gate-score quartiles, comply rises
  $.42 \to .67 \to .75 \to .75$; resistance (pushback + lip-service) is
  .25 in the most-confident quartile vs.\ .08 elsewhere. A model that
  knows it doesn't know is willing to listen --- the gate and the relay
  are two uses of one signal. In the high-score half, hard-knowledge (the
  dominant escalation type) complies $1.00$ ($n{=}13$).
\item \textbf{Authority framing works; forcing words into the talker's
  mouth backfires.} Worst case (confident-correct model $\times$
  conflicting injection): F0 comply .00, F1 .53, F2 .79 --- but F3
  \emph{seeding} yields comply .25 with lip-service .62: the model speaks
  the seeded answer, then walks it back in its own continuation. Forced
  speech is treated as revisable; framing, not ventriloquism, is the
  mechanism. The deployed loop keeps F1 (F2 is the shelf option: it only
  helps a rare corner and plausibly raises the swallow rate below).
\item \textbf{Math is silently re-derived.} ``Other'' concentrates in
  hard-math: the model neither relays nor disputes --- it re-derives and
  states its own number (comply .57 / silent .26 vs.\ .66 / .20
  non-math). A bare numeric answer carries no authority against the
  model's own computation chain; harmless when the expert is right, but
  verbatim-relay claims are off the table for math.
\item \textbf{The swallow rate sets the ceiling.} Model-wrong $\times$
  wrong-injection: comply .64, pushback .00. An unconfident model has no
  internal resistance to a wrong expert --- expert quality is the
  accuracy ceiling of the whole cascade, now quantified.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The live curve, with the deployment price itemized}
\label{sec:livecurve}

With the channel verified clean, we ran the full live sweep: 240 frozen
test queries $\times$ four tiers (never / conservative / balanced /
aggressive), each a complete duplex session with the deployed v3 probe
(the multi-position L22 refit of \S\ref{sec:transfer}), scored on
\emph{heard accuracy} --- the judge sees exactly what a user would have
heard (the relay for escalated queries, the local streaming answer
otherwise). Labels are frozen in \texttt{frozen\_v3\_traces.parquet};
all statistics are paired bootstrap over queries on those labels
(Table~\ref{tab:live}, Figure~\ref{fig:dualview}).

Headline numbers are reported on the \textbf{speakable subset}
($n{=}218$): a pre-registered input-side fairness filter --- decidable
from query text and wav bytes alone, never from outcomes --- removes 21
formula-LaTeX ids and one digitally-silent wav from the 240. Formula
content read aloud by a TTS is destroyed before the system under test
ever hears it (no one speaks LaTeX at a voice assistant), and the
exclusion is asymmetric in exactly the way the artifact predicts: the
gold-inject ceiling barely moves (.917 $\to$ .922) while the aggressive
channel gap drops .175 $\to$ .128 --- LaTeX rows destroy the speech
channel specifically, they are not intrinsically harder for the expert.
Rare entities and hard names are deliberately \emph{not} filtered:
mishearing them is the phenomenon. Full-pool rows appear in
Table~\ref{tab:live} for comparison.

The curve rises monotonically and every tier's gain over the live floor
is significant: +.106/+.170/+.211 on the speakable subset, +.21 at 61\%
escalation on the full pool. Selectivity, however, must be read in the
channel-controlled view. In the heard view the higher tiers sit at or
below a random-escalation reference drawn to the gold ceiling
(aggressive .633 vs.\ .709): the speech-channel cost (+.128 at that
tier) eats the routing margin, so on this mixed pool escalating
\emph{at all} helps, but \emph{which} queries the gate picks is masked
by the uplink. Remove exactly that channel --- the gold-inject view ---
and the gate clears the same random reference at every arm
(+.059/+.083/+.052). The routing signal survives deployment; the
binding constraint at high escalation rates is the transcription
uplink, not the gate (the external pools of \S\ref{sec:transfer}, where
the channel is milder, show the heard-view advantage directly). One
deployment correction fell out of this analysis: re-deriving the tier
thresholds as label-free quantiles of the deployment-mix scores
(\texttt{gate\_v3\_thresholds\_corrected.json}) removes 11.3\% of the
expert calls at equal accuracy on this pool --- the drifted aggressive
threshold was a \emph{cost} bug, not an accuracy bug. The interesting
content is the \emph{decomposition} of the gap to the offline curve,
which the two views separate:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Audio + streaming-answer tax (floor .588 $\to$ .383 full
  pool, .422 speakable).} The
  live floor stacks the audio modality tax (\S\ref{sec:speech}) with a
  streaming-answer tax: in duplex voice mode the model produces short,
  speech-styled answers that sometimes plan aloud and stop. This is real
  deployment behavior --- it is exactly why the never-escalate endpoint
  had to be run live rather than synthesized from chat-mode replays.
\item \textbf{Speech-channel cost (+.023$^{\dagger}$/+.069/+.128 on the
  speakable subset; +.175 at aggressive on the full pool).} On the
  escalated population, the expert answering the talker's own
  transcript scores .585 vs.\ .871 on gold text ($n{=}147$ escalated
  ids). The un-speakable share of this loss is exactly what the
  fairness filter removes; the deployment-real residue is entity loss
  on hard-knowledge: prompt-level rescues on the self-transcript are
  no-ops (a recognition-errors warning changes nothing; $k$-best
  self-transcription trades a small trap gain for a larger knowledge
  loss) because the damaging errors are internally-coherent entity
  substitutions with no textual residue to correct from. Replacing the
  \emph{ears} is different from repairing the transcript: open-weights
  whisper-large-v3 recovers little (+4\,pp), but routing the same wavs
  through a stronger cloud ASR (\texttt{gpt-transcribe}) and handing
  \emph{that} text to the expert scores .694 vs.\ .585 (paired McNemar
  $p{=}.007$), recovering 38\% of the gap to the gold ceiling --- a
  diagnostic result for now: its latency on the critical path and its
  survival on the external pools are unmeasured (the input-side limit
  is stated in \S\ref{sec:limitations}). A trace-level
  decomposition of the escalated heard-failures (measured on the
  first-generation sweep; the claim is the mechanism, not the exact
  split) confirms the loss sits
  upstream of the relay: 64\% are corrupted transcripts (the expert
  answers the wrong question), 24\% are expert misses on clean
  transcripts (dominated by trap --- an expert knowledge limit shared
  with the gold arm), and only 5 cases show a correct expert answer
  dropped in relay --- consistent with the measured relay compliance
  of 1.00 on the deployed channel and with expert micro-turn answers
  being short (median 76 characters), which rules out talker context
  length as a mechanism.
  A final controlled arm closes the attribution: sending the
  \emph{original wavs} directly to an audio-capable expert
  (gpt-audio), with a same-model gold-text control to subtract its
  capability gap, shows the audio channel itself is lossless for
  speakable content --- trap and easy-fact lose zero crossing it, and
  the same recordings the talker garbled (``999 $-$ 103'' with the
  minus dropped; ``Mustafa Adebayo Balogun'') are heard verbatim by
  the stronger ear. The synthesis is therefore exonerated along with
  the relay; the leak is the talker's own transcription step. We treat
  ASR robustness as an orthogonal channel property, not a contribution
  of this system.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The replication-noise floor.} Live arm accuracies on a
200--250-query pool carry substantial judge-and-decode replication
noise, and we report it rather than narrate inside it: the same query,
same audio, kept \emph{local} in two different arms flips its judge
verdict 2.3--18.8\% of the time depending on pool (this pool: .155);
repeated \emph{escalation} flips 0.7--10.6\%. The implied paired SE on
an arm-vs-arm accuracy delta is .009--.028, so any live delta under
$\sim$3 points is undetectable in a single sweep --- accordingly, no
claim in this paper rests on a sub-3-point live difference; probe
comparisons rest on offline AUC and matched-rate precision, which are
far tighter statistics on the full score distribution.
The full audit is Figure~\ref{fig:app-noise}.

\paragraph{What escalation costs in wall-clock time.} The same frozen
traces carry per-session timers, so the latency price of each operating
point is measured, not modeled (Table~\ref{tab:latency}). The gate
itself is free at this scale: the end-of-turn read and the stall
prefill cost $\sim$30\,ms each at P50; the expert round-trip
(\textsc{gpt-5.5}, low effort; P50 4.8\,s, P95 20.8\,s) dominates
escalated-session wall time (escalated-session P50 5.1--7.5\,s vs.\
local 1.9--3.0\,s). The consequence is a specific distributional
signature: at the balanced tier the \emph{mean} response time rises
only +1.2\,s and the P50 +1.5\,s over the never floor, but the P99
nearly doubles (17.8\,$\to$\,32.7\,s) --- escalation is cheap on
average and expensive exactly in the expert-latency tail ($n=240$ per
arm, so P99 rests on the two worst sessions and is indicative only).
The conservative tier even \emph{shortens} the tail (P95
15.8\,$\to$\,12.8\,s): its few escalations displace exactly the
longest local decodes, the same median-of-a-mixture mechanism analyzed
on the external pools (\S\ref{sec:transfer}).
Figure~\ref{fig:timeline-live} replays three real balanced-arm sessions
from their recorded timers, showing the three shapes a session takes:
the expert beating the stall phrase (seamless handoff), the expert
outliving it (dead air --- the tail case above), and the gate staying
closed (first token in 0.4\,s).
Figure~\ref{fig:pareto-latency} joins the accuracy and latency readouts
into the exchange rate actually paid at the median (speakable subset):
+10.6 points of heard accuracy for +0.3\,s (never$\to$conservative),
+6.4 for +1.0\,s (conservative$\to$balanced), and +4.1 for +1.1\,s
(balanced$\to$aggressive). The first transition is both the steepest
and the cheapest per second, for two measured reasons: escalation
displaces local decodes that average 4.6\,s (and the conservative
tier's escalations are precisely the slowest of them, hence its P95
shortening above), while the marginal escalations added at higher
tiers reach easier queries whose experts return faster
(escalated-session P50 7.5/6.9/5.1\,s across the three tiers).

\subsection{Signal boundary: a third blind spot}
\label{sec:falseqa}

A fairness challenge to the trap pool --- SimpleQA entities are hard to
\emph{hear}, conflating knowledge difficulty with transcription
difficulty --- motivated auditioning a transcription-fair failure pool:
false-premise questions \citep{hu2023falseqa}, every word common by
design ($n=60$, criterion fixed before data selection). The audition
fails \emph{as a pool} and succeeds as a finding: the small model fails
substantially (.63 text / .47 audio) and the expert would help (.80
adequacy), but the end-of-turn gate is \textbf{blind} to these failures
--- fire@balanced .18 (vs.\ trap's .90), with essentially no score
separation between failed and passed answers (.568 vs.\ .507). A model
answering along a false premise does not ``feel'' unable: the mid-layer
signal reads \emph{missing knowledge}, not \emph{broken question}. This
is the third documented blind spot, after easy-chat failures and
decode-time math: the pre-answer signal is specifically a
\emph{retrieval-failure} detector. FalseQA therefore enters the
signal-boundary analysis, not the tradeoff pools.

\paragraph{A boundary case that data can move: real-time queries.}
A fourth query class surfaced in live demo use: questions whose answer
is unknowable at any training cutoff (``what is NVDA trading at right
now''). Unlike false premises, the gate is not blind here --- v3 scores
sit \emph{on} the balanced threshold, flipping with the TTS render
(.624 vs.\ .693 for the same question) --- the class was simply absent
from every (static-knowledge) training family. Extending the calibration
pool with FreshQA \citep{vu2023freshllms} closes it: fast-changing
questions (153, labeled escalate a priori --- the current value is
unknowable by construction, so a judge against a stale gold would only
add noise) plus never-changing in-family controls (150, standard
judge labels, fail rate .47). Refit with the frozen v3 feature config,
the v4 probe fires on held-out fast-changing questions at
.80/.93/1.0 across tiers (v3: .57/.80/1.0) while never-changing
held-out rates and frozen-test AUC are unchanged (.877 vs.\ .879).
One calibration subtlety: tier budgets must remain quantiles of the
\emph{deployment-mix} scores; quantiling over the augmented train mix
(123 extra all-positive rows) silently raised the balanced threshold
from .680 to .737, spending the new capability on its own threshold
shift. Escalation only pays for this class if the expert can reach live
data --- the demo's expert therefore gains a web-search tool, while all
measured arms keep the tool-free expert.
