\section{Transfer Without Training: The Frozen Gate on Six Public Speech Benchmarks}
\label{sec:transfer}

Everything so far is measured on our internal pool, and \S\ref{sec:audit}
showed exactly why that is not enough: in a mixed pool, most of a
router's apparent margin can be the pool/type shortcut. This section is
the out-of-distribution test the audit demands. We take the gate
\emph{frozen} --- probe weights, feature configuration, and layer choice
fit once on the internal calibration mix --- and run the complete live
streaming loop of \S\ref{sec:live} on six public speech-QA benchmarks:
Speech TriviaQA, Speech Web Questions, Llama Questions, and Reasoning QA
from OpenAudioBench \citep{openaudiobench2025} --- spoken renderings of
TriviaQA \citep{joshi2017triviaqa}, WebQuestions
\citep{berant2013webquestions}, and the Llama Questions set of
\citet{nachmani2024spoken} --- SD-QA
(real dialectal human speech, not TTS) \citep{faisal2021sdqa}, and
VoiceBench AlpacaEval \citep{chen2024voicebench, taori2023alpaca}. All
pools use the benchmarks' official pre-rendered audio --- none of it
passes through our TTS --- and none of their queries ever touches probe
training. The deployed probe (v3) is the same L22 end-of-turn read plus
two additional zero-latency positions (a rolling 8-token tail and the
running user-audio mean), refit on a calibration mix widened to 2{,}310
queries drawn from public families (SVAMP, HotpotQA, PopQA, MMLU,
TruthfulQA-style traps) that are disjoint from --- and deduplicated
against --- the six evaluation benchmarks; the pre-registered guard held
with headroom (frozen internal test AUC .860$\to$.879, and feature
selection never saw the externals). The only per-pool adaptation is
\emph{label-free}: escalation-tier thresholds are quantiles of the
probe's own score distribution on each pool, a normalization the score
scale forces (the aggressive threshold is .111 on Llama Questions vs.\
.603 on SD-QA --- a 5$\times$ shift for the same probe) and one that
consumes no labels, no answers, and no judge calls.

Scoring is each benchmark's own where a protocol exists: the three
OpenAudioBench pools use the official judge verbatim (alias-match
grading), AlpacaEval uses VoiceBench's official 1--5 judge, and SD-QA /
Reasoning QA (which ship no replicable protocol) use our
reference-anchored judge. Under the official judge our offline chat-mode
control \emph{reproduces} the anchors --- .712 vs.\ the official .755 on
Speech TriviaQA (250-item subsample + protocol) and .716 vs.\ .702 on
Web Questions --- so the official lines in our figures are on-scale, and
the live floor's remaining gap to them is fully accounted (striviaqa:
$-$.048 streaming-loop tax, $-$.043 subsample/protocol; nothing
unexplained).

\subsection{Accuracy: the mechanism ports, and the anchors fall}
\label{sec:transfer-acc}

Table~\ref{tab:transfer} is the headline: the frozen gate transfers
mechanically everywhere --- thresholds fire at sane rates, every curve
rises monotonically, and the 50\%-escalation arm lifts the live floor by
+.11 to +.29 across the five QA pools. Three anchor results:

\textbf{Routing beats scaling the speech backbone.} On Speech TriviaQA
the 9B duplex model plus the frozen gate at 50\% escalation scores
\textbf{.860 live}, against .755 for MiniCPM-o 4.5's own official
offline number and \textbf{.629} for Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B
\citep{qwen3omni2025} at 3.3$\times$ the parameters (offline chat
mode; Kimi-Audio, \citealp{kimiaudio2025}, scores .419 on the same
table). On Web Questions
the gated system clears the official number (.732 vs.\ .702) and lands
just under Qwen3-Omni (.749).

\textbf{On Llama Questions, selective escalation matches the expert at
half the cost.} The pool is single-type (short English factoids), so no
type shortcut exists to exploit, and the local model is strong (floor
.84). The probe still splits it cleanly: at 50\% the kept-local half
scores \textbf{.976} vs.\ \textbf{.696} on the escalated half
($z{=}6.46$) --- genuine per-query discrimination --- and the expert's
entire advantage concentrates where the probe sends it (escalated half
.696$\to$.888, McNemar $p{<}.0001$) while buying nothing measurable on
the kept half (.976 vs.\ .968, $p{=}1.00$). Selective at 50\% reaches
.948 vs.\ .928 for always-escalate (directionally above, $p{=}.125$ ---
both near ceiling): escalating everything spends twice the expert calls
to buy nothing measurable over the gate.

\textbf{Router quality must be read as AUC plus matched-rate precision,
not raw precision.} Precision at a fixed escalation rate is capped at
base-fail/rate: on Llama Questions (base fail .16) the cap at 50\% is
.32, and the v3 gate's matched-rate precision of .304 is \textbf{95\% of
the theoretical maximum} (recall .93); quoting ``.30 precision'' without
the cap would misread the strongest pool as the weakest. The
scale-free readout is transfer AUC against the never-arm fail label:
.68--.81 on every external pool (Table~\ref{tab:transfer}) vs.\ .879 on
the internal held-out test --- the mid-layer signal ports as a generic
difficulty signal, with a near-uniform matched-rate precision lift of
$\times$1.33--1.35 across pools rather than pool-specific structure.
The cleanest evidence that the signal is difficulty, not surface, is
cross-lingual: the calibration expansion is \emph{English-only}, yet it
moves Reasoning QA --- a \emph{Chinese} execution-reasoning pool ---
from AUC .621 to \textbf{.683} (+.062), the largest per-pool gain of the
refit. Per the replication-noise analysis below, the v3 evidence is
this offline AUC shift; the corresponding live-arm deltas
(+.010--.030) are individually within noise and we do not narrate them.

The latency curves of Figure~\ref{fig:transfer-pareto} have a shape
worth explaining honestly, because at first sight it looks like a
measurement error: on the easy pools the per-arm P50 is
\emph{non-monotonic} --- on Llama Questions it \emph{falls} from 1.52\,s
(never) to 1.17\,s at 15\% escalation before rising again
(1.60/1.42\,s at 30/50\%). This left-fold is a verified
median-of-a-mixture mechanism, not noise: the probe preferentially
escalates exactly the queries whose \emph{local} decode is longest.
On Speech TriviaQA the 38 queries escalated at 15\% had a never-arm
local P50 of 1.711\,s vs.\ 1.198\,s for the 212 kept --- and, measured
in the never arm, local accuracy .32 vs.\ .73: the small model's
signature failure mode on retrieval pools is hedging at length, so
wrong answers are slow answers (decode time and answer length are the
same variable, $r{=}.97$), and a wrongness-selecting probe strips the
slow tail of the local mixture as a side effect. Controls: the probe
does not key on length directly (corr(score, length) $= +.05$;
conditioned on wrongness the score--length coefficient is $\approx 0$),
and on SD-QA --- where wrong answers happen to be short and confident
--- the same probe selects wrongness identically and produces
\emph{no} fold. Figure~\ref{fig:transfer-decomp} decomposes the effect:
the fold lives only in the median --- the \emph{mean} is monotonic
(1.65/1.81/1.96/2.06\,s vs.\ P50 1.52/1.17/1.60/1.42\,s) --- and the
kept-local P50 falls 1.52$\to$0.96$\to$0.86$\to$0.43\,s as the probe
strips slow decodes while escalated rows pay a flat $\sim$3\,s expert
round-trip. We report the mean alongside the median for exactly this
reason; the honest statement is that on these pools escalation buys
accuracy \emph{and}, at the median, speed. The individual dip depth is
within noise (striviaqa $-$41\,ms, bootstrap CI $[-285,+254]$\,ms) but
its sign is deterministic --- identical probe scores select the
identical escalated set on every rerun.

On Reasoning QA the shape inverts usefully: P50 rises gently but the
\emph{tail improves} --- P90 drops from 13.25\,s to 10.94\,s under
escalation. The mechanism is that the local model's chain-of-thought is
\emph{spoken}, so every reasoning token enters the response clock
(escalated-set local decodes P50 4.38\,s, tail 13\,s+), while the
expert's chain is hidden: its median completion is 144 tokens of which
only 57 characters return, behind a flat $\sim$3.7\,s round-trip. On
reasoning pools the gate replaces the longest spoken chains with short
round-trips --- escalation truncates the latency tail rather than
fattening it.

\subsection{Where transfer fails, and what the live curves can and
cannot show}
\label{sec:transfer-neg}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=.9\linewidth]{sllama_latency_decomp}
  \caption{Decomposition of the Llama Questions latency fold. Left: the
  fold exists only in the median --- the mean is monotonic. Right:
  kept-local P50 falls 1.52$\to$0.43\,s across tiers as the probe strips
  the slowest local decodes, while escalated rows pay a flat $\sim$3\,s
  expert round-trip; the arm median folds left and then rises as the
  expert mass reaches it.}
  \label{fig:transfer-decomp}
\end{figure}

\textbf{AlpacaEval is a negative result, mechanism measured.} On
VoiceBench AlpacaEval the gate does \emph{not} beat random: aggressive
4.35 vs.\ random-at-50\% $\approx$4.45. The failure is selection, not
escalation --- queries the gate selected score 3.90 on the never arm
vs.\ 3.98 for those it skipped (no discrimination), while escalated rows
still gain (3.90$\to$4.71) purely because \textsc{gpt-5.5} writes better
long-form answers; random picks would harvest the same gain. Open-ended
instruction following has no ``the model lacks this fact'' event for a
retrieval-failure detector to read, and the score range is compressed
(1.0 point vs.\ 40 accuracy points on our pool). Two scale caveats make
the absolute level honest: the official 4.8 is an \emph{offline
chat-mode} number --- our chat-mode control reproduces it at 4.86 ---
and the entire chat-vs-live gap (4.86 vs.\ 3.99) is the duplex loop's
spoken-reply style plus a 512-token cap, so only the live arms are
comparable to each other.

\textbf{Web Questions and SD-QA are flat under the v3 refit}, offline
(AUC +.006/+.017) and live alike. On Web Questions a specific share of
the blame is label fidelity rather than the model: its strict
Freebase-alias grading decouples ``wrong'' from ``uncertain'' --- the
wrong-answers-are-longer signature is null there for every model we
measured, including \textsc{gpt-5.5} on gold text --- so a portion of
its labels is protocol-wrong that no state-reading probe can predict,
and even the gold-text expert ceiling is .844.

\textbf{The deployed view understates selection on easy pools.} The
external channel cost is small --- the aggressive-arm heard-vs-gold-inject
gap is .040/.024/.025 on striviaqa/swebq/sdqa
\todonote{verify: gold-inject gaps on the v3 traces; quoted values are
from the v2-probe sweep} vs.\ .175 on the internal pool (.128 on its
speakable subset, \S\ref{sec:livecurve}), exactly as predicted for short open questions
with no option walls --- but on easy homogeneous pools even a small tax
eats a thin per-query margin, and part of the deployed-view
``gate $\approx$ random'' on Speech TriviaQA is channel cost: in the
channel-controlled gold view the gate clears its paired random line
(+.044 at the aggressive arm). SD-QA meanwhile carries the one property
no TTS pool has --- real dialectal human speech --- and the escalated
subset still converts at .82--.84 with expert P50 3.1--3.4\,s: the
payload survives real speech.

\textbf{Replication-noise discipline.} Re-running the same query with
the same audio through the live loop flips the judge verdict 2.3--18.8\%
of the time depending on pool (Llama Questions .023, SD-QA .188),
implying a paired SE of .009--.028 on any arm-vs-arm accuracy delta.
Against that floor, all 18 per-arm live v2$\to$v3 deltas are
individually non-significant (the largest, striviaqa balanced +.036,
gives McNemar $p{=}.16$). We therefore treat sub-3-point live deltas as
unresolvable in a single sweep and rest every probe-improvement claim
in this section on the variance-reduced instruments --- offline AUC over
the full score distribution and matched-rate precision --- with the
live curves serving as the end-to-end existence proof, not the
measurement.
