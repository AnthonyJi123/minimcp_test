\section{Frozen Transfer to Public Speech Benchmarks}
\label{sec:transfer}
\label{sec:transfer-neg}

The out-of-distribution test the type-shortcut audit demands: we take
the deployed gate \emph{frozen} --- weights, features, and layer choice
fit once on the internal calibration mix --- and run the complete live
loop of \S\ref{sec:live} on six public speech benchmarks: Speech
TriviaQA, Speech Web Questions, Llama Questions, and Reasoning QA from
OpenAudioBench \citep{openaudiobench2025} (spoken renderings of
\citealp{joshi2017triviaqa}, \citealp{berant2013webquestions}, and the
set of \citealp{nachmani2024spoken}), SD-QA (real dialectal human
speech) \citep{faisal2021sdqa}, and VoiceBench AlpacaEval
\citep{chen2024voicebench,taori2023alpaca}. All audio is the
benchmarks' official pre-rendered audio; no query ever touches probe
calibration. The only per-pool adaptation is \emph{label-free}: tier
thresholds are quantiles of the probe's own score distribution on each
pool, consuming no labels and no judge calls. It buys budget control,
not discrimination: one absolute threshold frozen on the internal pool
still beats matched-rate random on four of five pools, but fires at
2--48\% across them (Appendix~\ref{app:fixedthr}). Where an official
protocol exists we score with it (the OpenAudioBench judge;
VoiceBench's 1--5 judge), and our chat-mode control reproduces the
official anchors on-scale.

\textbf{The frozen gate improves all five QA benchmarks}
(Table~\ref{tab:transfer}): thresholds fire at sane rates, every curve
rises monotonically, and 50\% escalation lifts the floor +.11--.29.
On Speech TriviaQA the 9B system reaches \textbf{.860 live}
--- against its own official offline .755 and .629 for Qwen3-Omni-30B
\citep{qwen3omni2025} at $3.3\times$ the parameters: selective cloud
escalation outperforms larger standalone backbones. AUC transfers at
.68--.81 against .879 internally, and the cleanest evidence that the
signal is difficulty rather than surface is cross-lingual: an
English-only calibration expansion moved \emph{Chinese} Reasoning QA
from AUC .621 to .683, the largest per-pool gain. On Llama Questions
--- single-type, so no shortcut to exploit --- \textbf{selective
escalation matches the expert at half the expert calls}: the probe's
split is decisive (kept-local .976 vs.\ escalated .696, $z{=}6.46$),
the expert's whole advantage lands where the probe sends it
(.696$\to$.888, $p{<}.0001$), and always-escalate buys nothing
measurable over selective; matched-rate precision reaches 95\% of the
base-rate cap (Appendix~\ref{app:transferdetail}).

Two honest negatives. \textbf{AlpacaEval}: the gate does not beat
random --- open-ended instruction following has no ``missing fact''
event for a retrieval-failure detector to read.
\textbf{Web Questions and SD-QA are flat} under the deployed-probe
refit: on Web Questions strict alias-based labels decouple ``wrong''
from ``uncertain'' (even the gold-text ceiling is .864), and the
failures the gate ranks highest are ones the expert misses too
(expert-fixable share .66 at the 15\% cut vs.\ .75 pool-wide). The external
channel cost is small (heard-vs-gold gap .035--.056 at aggressive vs.\
.175 internally), and SD-QA's escalated subset converts at .82--.84 on
real dialectal speech. Routing can also \emph{reduce} tail latency,
replacing slow wrong local decodes with short expert round-trips
(Reasoning QA P90 13.3$\to$10.9\,s; Appendix~\ref{app:transferdetail}).
