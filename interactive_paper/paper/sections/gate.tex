\section{From Signal to Gate}
\label{sec:system}

\paragraph{The decision exists before decoding starts.}
The gate is one 4096-d dot product on a hidden state the forward pass
already computes. Measured on H100, the L22 decision lands in
\textbf{20\,ms (text) / 45\,ms (audio)} against time-to-first-token of
36/68\,ms: $T_{\text{gate}} < T_{\text{first token}}$, far inside the
200--300\,ms turn-taking budget \citep{stivers2009universals}, and the
cloud call can launch while the remaining $\sim$40\% of prefill and the
whole decode still run ($\ptrue$-pre costs an extra prefill,
$\ptrue$-post a full draft: 1.9/3.5\,s; per-layer cost curves in
Appendix~\ref{app:gatedetail}).

\paragraph{Accuracy--cost tradeoff.}
On the frozen test split ($n{=}240$): small-only 58.8\%, big-only
(\texttt{gpt-5.5}) 91.7\%. Gated escalation beats random escalation at
every operating point (Figure~\ref{fig:tradeoff}, appendix); the
balanced tier at a 33\% budget reaches \textbf{77.9--78.7\%}, recovering
$\sim$58\% of the small$\to$big gap at one third of the big-only cost.
In distribution the gate is statistically on par with a pool-oracle
(type-only) router --- as the type-shortcut audit predicts --- so its
case over type routing rests on transfer, where the type shortcut
collapses (\S\ref{sec:audit}) and the mid-layer signal does not.

\paragraph{Against the model's own thinking mode.}
\label{sec:thinking}
MiniCPM-o~4.5 ships a built-in reasoning mode --- self-escalation in
place. Gated-cloud escalation dominates all-THINK on both accuracy and
latency at every escalation rate (gated-cloud@33\%: 78.7\% at mean 6.5\,s
vs.\ all-THINK 63.7\% at 22.4\,s; Table~\ref{tab:policy}). The reason is
mechanistic: the gate predominantly flags knowledge and trap failures,
which extra compute cannot fix --- thinking helps math and hurts chat
--- so external escalation is necessary, not merely better.
