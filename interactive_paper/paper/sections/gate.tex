\section{The Read Arrives Before Response Commitment}
\label{sec:system}

\paragraph{Scoring latency.}
The gate is a linear read on L22 states the forward pass already
computes --- a 4096-d dot product per position, over one position
(mechanistic probe) or three (the deployed probe adds two running
means at no added scoring latency; Appendix~\ref{app:signal}).
On an H100, measured from prefill start with CUDA synchronization, the
score is available at P50 in \textbf{20\,ms for text and 45\,ms for
audio} (P95 25/104\,ms). Time to first token on the same benchmark is
36/68\,ms. The measurement excludes network time, but it establishes
the ordering needed by the system: the cloud request can start before
local decoding commits to an answer. Waiting for decoded tokens does
not improve the read in our data. Max entropy over four tokens reaches
AUC 0.696, and early-decode hidden states reach 0.776, compared with
0.828 at the prompt; waiting also postpones the expert request
(Appendix~\ref{app:gatedetail}).

\paragraph{From score to escalation budget.}
On the frozen test split ($n{=}240$), always-local accuracy is 58.8\%
and always-expert accuracy is 91.7\%. Thresholding the probe beats
matched-rate random escalation at every operating point
(Figure~\ref{fig:tradeoff}, appendix). At a 33\% escalation budget, it
reaches \textbf{77.9--78.7\%}, recovering about 58\% of the
local-to-expert gap with one third of the expert calls. In
distribution, the gate is statistically tied with a pool-identity
oracle, as the shortcut audit predicts. Its advantage appears under
transfer, where pool identity stops being sufficient
(\S\ref{sec:audit}).

The gate is fit to \emph{local failure}, not expert benefit: a benefit
label would specialize the ranking to the current expert. The frozen
scores remain useful under the stricter benefit label, but pools with
little expert headroom remain a system-level limit
(Appendix~\ref{app:fixedthr}). Comparisons with the model's built-in
reasoning mode are in Appendix~\ref{app:gatedetail}.
