\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A full-duplex speech model listens and speaks in the same forward pass,
under a conversational deadline of a few hundred milliseconds
\citep{stivers2009universals}. That deadline forces a small on-device
backbone --- and a small backbone routinely faces queries beyond its
competence: long-tail facts, expert knowledge, multi-step reasoning. The
natural architecture is selective escalation to a large cloud model, but
the duplex setting imposes two constraints that existing routing work
does not meet: the decision must come \emph{before the first output
token} (once the model starts speaking, a wrong answer is already on the
air), and the session --- audio context, voice, cache --- must stay with
the local model, which will speak the expert's answer itself.

This paper asks whether the small model already contains the signal this
decision needs. We study MiniCPM-o~4.5 \citep{openbmb2026minicpmo}, a 9B
omni/duplex fine-tune of Qwen3-8B, against matched-pair controls
spanning raw backbones, streaming-omni, audio-only, and frozen-adapter
fine-tunes, and evaluate every standard zero-shot competence readout ---
decode-time entropy, verbalized self-assessment ($\ptrue$), and linear
probes on hidden states --- with judged failure labels on a frozen pool
of public-benchmark queries, in matched text and speech conditions.

Everything runs on a frozen checkpoint --- no fine-tuning; the only
fitted component is a logistic regression on stored hidden states,
calibrated once on text. Three findings, in increasing order of
system-level consequence:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Representation: duplex fine-tuning breaks the standard
  readouts; a mid-network signal survives.} The conventional
  final-layer probe looks strong in distribution (AUC .82) but is
  mostly a query-type shortcut: under leave-one-pool-out transfer it
  \emph{inverts} on held-out math (.93$\to$.37). The damage is specific
  to the late-layer last-token position on text input --- absent in raw
  backbones and non-duplex fine-tunes --- consistent with late-layer
  specialization for duplex turn control. Mid-network (L22 of 36) the
  same linear read transfers at .93, and the mid-network optimum
  generalizes to a second, architecturally disjoint duplex family
  (hybrid-Mamba NemotronLabs-VoiceChat-11B).
\item \textbf{Modality: the mid-layer signal transfers text
  $\to$ speech in end-to-end models.} A probe calibrated on cheap text
  data reads speech-input hidden states at .86; the control that
  isolates the mechanism is Freeze-Omni, an adapter on frozen identical
  weights, where cross-modal transfer is dead ($\le$.60). Verbalized
  self-assessment shows the mirror-image failure --- it collapses on
  audio input --- so the mid-layer probe is the one readout that
  survives both modalities. The transfer holds on real human
  recordings, not just TTS.
\item \textbf{System: the signal is available before the first token
  and works live, in and out of distribution.} The L22 read costs
  20\,ms (text) / 45\,ms (audio) against time-to-first-token of
  36/68\,ms; in the streaming loop an end-of-turn read reaches AUC
  .887 in $\sim$30\,ms. Run live end-to-end --- expert consulted
  mid-conversation, answer injected, talker speaks it --- heard
  accuracy climbs .422$\to$.633 at 57\% escalation, and the frozen
  gate transfers to six public speech benchmarks with label-free
  thresholds, lifting Speech TriviaQA to .860 against the model's
  official offline .755.
\end{enumerate}
