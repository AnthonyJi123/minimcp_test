\section{Reading Competence from Duplex Models}
\label{sec:signal}

\subsection{In distribution every readout looks fine; transfer reveals
the shortcut}
\label{sec:audit}

Table~\ref{tab:signals} compares the candidate signals on
MiniCPM-o~4.5. In distribution the final-layer probe reaches 0.822 ---
but that number is mostly a \emph{query-type} shortcut: the same hidden
state classifies a query's source pool at 95.8\%, a pool-identity
oracle alone reaches AUC 0.715, and appending true pool labels to the
probe adds nothing (0.822$\to$0.821). The decisive test is
leave-one-pool-out (LOPO) transfer, which we treat as the primary
metric throughout: held-out math \emph{inverts} to 0.372, and the mean
escalation score on a 100\%-fail trap pool is 0.232. An external
query-surface router makes the same point from the other side: under
identical labels a TF-IDF router reaches only OOF AUC 0.669 (below the
pool oracle), collapses to 0.38--0.57 under LOPO, and $100\times$ the
data on a public routing benchmark buys back only 0.04 of AUC
(Appendix~\ref{app:router}). Instance-level competence information
lives in the model's internal state, not on the query surface.

Verbalized self-assessment transfers differently: \emph{pre-answer}
$\ptrue$ catches the trap pool the probe misses (escalation score 0.945
vs.\ the probe's 0.232) --- but timing is essential: asked \emph{after}
drafting, the model endorses its own confident-wrong answer (0.360).
Post-draft $\ptrue$ is the strongest single signal (0.899) but requires
paying for a full draft, which a voice deadline cannot afford.

\subsection{The damage is duplex-specific; the signal survives
mid-network}
\label{sec:duplex}

Matched pairs attribute the probe failure to the \emph{kind} of
fine-tune, not the backbone: the raw Qwen backbone, label-matched to the
duplex model's fail rates, holds LOPO math at 0.825 where the duplex
model sits at 0.372; streaming-omni and audio-only fine-tunes are
undamaged; a second duplex generation (MiniCPM-o~2.6) reproduces the
collapse (Appendix~\ref{app:signal}). A layer $\times$ position sweep
localizes it (Figure~\ref{fig:layersweep}): the collapse is specific to
(late layer $\times$ last token) on text input --- mean-pooled reads
survive at the same depth but 0.13--0.15 below the mid-layer peak
(LOPO math $\approx$0.80 here, 0.674 on MiniCPM-o~2.6), and
mid-network the last-token read recovers to \textbf{0.931 at L22},
near the raw backbone. This is the position a
streaming head must re-purpose to encode turn-control state, and the
pattern is consistent with late-layer specialization for duplex turn
control overwriting the readout while the underlying information
survives. The mid-network optimum is not a MiniCPM quirk: on
NemotronLabs-VoiceChat-11B --- a hybrid-Mamba duplex model,
architecturally disjoint --- the same recipe peaks mid-band at L30--34
of 56 and transfers to the external pools at AUC 0.70--0.79 with a
quarter of the calibration data (Appendix~\ref{app:nvda}).

\subsection{Text-calibrated, speech-deployed --- in end-to-end models
only}
\label{sec:xmodal}

Probes trained on text hidden states and scored on speech-input hidden
states locate where the representation becomes modality-shared: early
layers are modality-specific (0.54--0.60), and from $\sim$50\% depth
text$\to$audio transfer plateaus at 0.82--0.87 (0.855 at the deployed
L22). This replicates on MiniCPM-o~2.6 and cross-family on
Qwen2.5-Omni. The decisive control is \textbf{Freeze-Omni}: identical
frozen LLM weights with audio attached by an adapter --- and
cross-modal transfer is dead (0.52--0.60). The modality-shared mid-layer
core is \emph{created by training the backbone on the modality}; the
cheap recipe ``calibrate on text, deploy on speech'' is valid for
end-to-end models and invalid for adapter-attached ones. Verbalized
introspection shows the mirror-image failure: on audio input,
pre-answer $\ptrue$ collapses on both MiniCPM duplex generations while
both non-duplex controls stay intact; controlled arms show the verbal
self-check runs over text-token pathways that audio embeddings do not
feed --- duplicating the question as text restores it --- while a
linear probe reads the same information off the same forward pass at
0.93 (Appendix~\ref{app:signal}). All audio-side findings replicate on
200 real human recordings (SD-QA), ruling out TTS artifacts.
\textbf{The mid-layer probe is the one readout that survives every
training regime that can converse}; the live system deploys the same
L22 read plus two zero-latency pooled positions (the \emph{deployed}
probe; Appendix~\ref{app:signal}).
